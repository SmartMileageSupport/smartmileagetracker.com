Mileage tracking for taxes, answered.

How do I track mileage for taxes?

The IRS lets you deduct qualifying miles at a standard rate it sets for each year — but the deduction has to survive on your records: the date, the distance, and the business purpose of each drive. Smart Mileage Tracker captures the date, route, distance, and addresses automatically for every drive, gives Business Purpose its own field, and exports it all as a CSV or PDF your accountant can use.

Does automatic mileage tracking drain my battery?

Smart Mileage uses your location in the background so drives can be detected automatically, and background GPS does use some battery. Sampling adapts to your speed to keep the cost low, and you can restrict automatic tracking to when your iPhone is plugged in — useful if your car charges your phone anyway.

Is Smart Mileage Tracker really free?

Yes — free on the App Store, with no account to create and no subscription. Tracking is unlimited: there is no cap on trips. If paid extras ever arrive, they will be a one-time purchase — never a subscription.

Which drives are tax deductible?

More than business alone: the IRS publishes standard mileage rates for business, medical, and charitable driving, and Smart Mileage prices all three — each at the rate for the year the trip happened. Regular commuting generally is not deductible.

Deduction figures in the app are estimates, not tax advice; confirm your situation with a tax professional.

Where does my trip data go?

Nowhere. Your trips live in a local database on your iPhone — there is no account and no server of ours for them to be uploaded to. Street-address lookups use Apple's Maps service; the log itself stays with you, and you can back it up to a JSON file you own. Privacy policy.
